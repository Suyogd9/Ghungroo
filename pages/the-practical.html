Essay No. 03

The boring half, made less boring

Visa, channel, studies, and the path — explained the way I'd explain them to someone I love. You said "bhai he sagle maza dokyavarun jaate" once. This is the version that doesn't.

Part one

Where I stand, and what it means for you

For the rest of this essay to make sense, you need three facts about my current immigration situation. I'll keep it short. Anything we actually file will go through my company's immigration lawyer first — this is just so you have the lay of the land.

Right Now
I'm on an H-1B — a work visa. Your H-4 will be attached to mine. "Dependent" is the technical word; in practice it means the H-1B is the spine and the H-4 follows wherever it goes.
February 2026
I-140 process started, currently in PWD. PWD stands for Prevailing Wage Determination — step one of the green card sequence. The full order: PWD → PERM → I-140 filing → I-140 approval. One to two years total, depending on the year and the lawyers and the gods.
The Unlock
Once I-140 is approved, H-4 EAD becomes available. EAD = Employment Authorization Document. Until then, your H-4 has work restrictions. After it, you can do basically anything. We'll get to the workarounds in a moment.

The H-4, plainly

An H-4 is a dependent visa. It lets the spouse of someone on H-1B live legally in the US for as long as the H-1B is valid. It comes with a particular set of capabilities, and a particular set of restrictions — both of which are more workable than they sound.

What you can do
  • Live in the US legally, for as long as I'm here
  • Travel in and out of the country freely
  • Open bank accounts, get an SSN or ITIN
  • Get a driver's license
  • Study — full-time or part-time, undergraduate or graduate
  • Volunteer in unpaid roles
What you can't (yet)
  • Earn employment income from a US employer
  • Monetize a US-based channel for revenue
  • Operate a US-registered business as your livelihood
  • File as self-employed in the US

The list on the right is the loud one, but it's narrower than it looks — and it disappears entirely the day the EAD comes through. Which brings us to the channel.

Part two

Your channel, in two phases

The short version: you can absolutely start, and you can absolutely keep going. You just can't earn from it yet. Here's how I'd think about it, in two stages.

Phase one — On H-4

Build the runway.

  • Create and publish freely — Instagram and YouTube both
  • Grow subscribers, watch hours, your library of recipes
  • Do not enable monetization: no AdSense, no paid sponsorships, no Super Chats
  • If you hit 1,000 subs and 4,000 watch hours — congratulations, but hold off on the Partner Program until EAD
  • Treat it as portfolio and audience-building, not income
Phase two — On H-4 EAD

Turn it on.

  • Enable YouTube Partner Program; AdSense begins paying
  • Accept paid sponsorships and brand collaborations
  • Sell merchandise, courses, ebooks, digital products
  • Affiliate links — Amazon Associates and the rest
  • Memberships and Super Thanks
  • Optional: form an LLC for tax and liability reasons
A US kitchen, sponsorships in USD, and a global audience already half-found. Phase two is where it gets interesting.

It's worth pointing out that channels like yours — Marathi food, told well, shot well — do measurably better from the US than from India, for unromantic reasons. The CPM (what YouTube pays per thousand views) is roughly three to five times higher for US-based viewers, which you'll have more of. Brand sponsorships pay in dollars. The production ceiling is higher because the kitchens, the ingredients, and the light are all different. And "Indian food in the US" is a genuinely lucrative niche because of who watches it.

There is one gray area — running the channel through accounts based outside the US, with revenue routed to an Indian bank account. Some couples do this. The rules are fuzzy. We'll get proper legal advice before making any move there. Not the kind of thing to improvise.

Part three

Studying — the unexpectedly good option

I want to make a case for something you might not have considered: enrolling in a US program during the H-4 window. The case rests on three points.

  1. You're already eligible. H-4 lets you enroll full-time or part-time at any accredited US university. No special permission needed.
  2. You'll qualify for in-state tuition. Once we've lived in a state for about twelve months (the exact rules vary), you're treated as a resident for tuition purposes. In-state rates are typically two to four times cheaper than out-of-state. This matters more than people realize.
  3. You have a clean exit ramp called F-1 → OPT. In your final semester, you can switch from H-4 to F-1 (student visa). Graduation unlocks OPT — Optional Practical Training — twelve months of legal work in your field, or thirty-six months if the program is STEM-designated. This means you'd have a credentialed path to a US job even if EAD takes longer than expected. Two paths to the same destination.

Programs that fit you, specifically

Given your design background, your channel, and your eye for visuals, the natural candidates are below. STEM-designated programs (marked) extend OPT from twelve to thirty-six months — a meaningful difference.

UX / UI / HCI
Many are STEM. Your portfolio is your leg up. Direct career fit, designer-led culture in the US is strong.

Storytelling, video production, journalism. Directly applicable to your channel work.

Audience growth, branding, sponsorship strategy. Turns the channel into a real business.

Part four

Two paths, one destination

Here is the part I want you to remember above all else: you have two independent roads to working freely in the US. They run in parallel. You can travel both at the same time. No path closes off another.

The EAD route
B
The student route
∞
Run both together

Path A — the EAD route

You arrive on H-4. We wait for my I-140 to approve (one to two years). You apply for the H-4 EAD (sixty to ninety days of processing). Once it lands, you can work, freelance, monetize, hire, sell — anything. This is the path most green-card-track families take.

Path B — the student route

You arrive on H-4. You apply to a program for the next intake. You establish state residency for in-state tuition. You enroll. In your final semester, you switch to F-1. Graduation hands you OPT — twelve months, or thirty-six if STEM. This path is entirely independent of my green card timeline.

The beautiful thing: you can run both at the same time.
Channel + studies + EAD application, in parallel.

A possible first year, just to make it concrete

Months 1–3
Settling in. Arrive on H-4, SSN/ITIN, driver's license, bank accounts. First US-shot videos. Scout out colleges and programs.
Months 4–6
Building. Apply to a program for next intake. Channel: consistent uploads. Establish state residency. Weekends spent exploring the country.
Months 7–9
Studying and creating. Coursework begins if admitted. Channel growing, monetization still off. Networking with other Indian creators in the US.
Months 10–12
Unlocking. I-140 likely approved around here. Apply for H-4 EAD immediately. Channel monetization switches on. Future you, unobstructed.
⁂

None of this is settled. All of it is workable. The point isn't that I've planned your life — it's that I've thought about it carefully, and the answers I've found make me more confident, not less, that this is a good move for both of us. The boring half turns out to be the half with the most opportunity. Which is encouraging.
