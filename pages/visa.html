Where I stand, and what it means for you.

Three facts about my situation, then we move on to the interesting parts.

Right Now
I'm on an H-1B a US work visa. Your H-4 will be attached to mine. "Dependent" is the technical word; in practice it means H-1B is the spine and H-4 follows.
Feb 2026
I-140 started, currently in PWD. PWD = Prevailing Wage Determination step one of the green-card sequence. Full order: PWD → PERM → I-140 filing → I-140 approval. 2 to 2.5 years total.
The Unlock
Once I-140 is approved, H-4 EAD becomes available. EAD = Employment Authorization Document. Until then, H-4 has work restrictions. After it, you can do basically anything.

The H-4, plainly.

It's a dependent visa. It lets the spouse of someone on H-1B live legally in the US for as long as the H-1B is valid. Specific capabilities and specific restrictions follow.

What you can do
  • Live in the US legally, as long as I'm here
  • Travel in and out of the country freely
  • Open bank accounts, get an SSN or ITIN
  • Get a driver's license
  • Study — full-time or part-time
  • Post videos, build an audience (more below)
  • Volunteer in unpaid roles
What you can't (yet)
  • Earn employment income from a US employer
  • Monetize a US-based channel for revenue
  • Operate a US-registered business as your livelihood
  • File as self-employed in the US

That second list looks bigger than it actually is. Almost everything on it disappears the day the EAD lands. Until then, two genuinely good options exist — and both compound nicely over time.

Your YouTube Channel — start now, monetize later.

Here's the thing most people miss about the H-4 restrictions on YouTube: they only stop you from earning money. They don't stop you from creating, posting, or building an audience. And building an audience is the part that actually takes time.

Think of it this way. The hardest part of any channel isn't the monetization toggle, that's a single click in YouTube Studio. The hardest part is the two years it usually takes to get to the point where the toggle is worth flipping. Most creators don't reach a substantial subscriber base for one to two years after starting consistently. So while EAD is processing, you'd be doing the slow, compounding work that pays off later anyway.

The reframe: the H-4 years aren't wasted years for your channel, they're the foundation years. The years it would take to grow regardless. The only difference is that when EAD arrives, you flip the switch and suddenly two years of quiet work starts paying.

Phase 01 — On H-4

Build the runway.

  • Create and publish freely — Instagram, YouTube, both
  • Grow subscribers, watch hours, your library
  • Do not enable monetization: no AdSense, no sponsorships, no Super Chats
  • Cross 1,000 subs / 4,000 hours? Hold the Partner Program off until EAD
  • Treat it as portfolio + audience, not income
Phase 02 — On EAD

Flip the switch.

  • Enable YouTube Partner Program → AdSense starts paying
  • Accept paid sponsorships and brand collaborations
  • Merchandise, courses, ebooks, digital products
  • Affiliate links — Amazon Associates and others
  • Memberships, Super Thanks, channel perks
  • Optional: form an LLC for tax and liability protection

And once Phase 02 begins, the math is genuinely better.. CPM (what YouTube pays per thousand views) is roughly three to five times higher for US-based viewers. Sponsorships pay in dollars. The production ceiling is higher because the kitchens, the ingredients, and the light are all different. "Indian food in the US" is a real, well-funded niche.

The other smart move — studying.

I want to make a case for something you might not have considered: enrolling in a US program during the H-4 window. The case is stronger than it sounds, and it rests on three compounding advantages.

1. Tuition drops to roughly one-third.

Once we've lived in a state for about twelve months, I have crossed 5 years in NC now. The rules vary slightly by state but you qualify as a state resident for tuition purposes. In-state rates are typically one-third to one-half of what international students pay. A program that costs $60,000 per program at international rates often costs $20,000 per program for state residents. This is not a small detail.

So the move is: arrive on H-4, settle, apply for the next intake, get charged in-state. The same degree, same diploma, same employers reading the resume, at a fraction of the cost.

2. The F-1 switch — your independent path to working.

Here's where it gets interesting. You don't have to stay on H-4 the whole time you're studying. In your final semester, you can switch your visa status from H-4 to F-1 — the student visa. That switch is what unlocks the next door.

Graduation on F-1 entitles you to OPT — Optional Practical Training. Twelve months of legal employment in your field, no employer sponsorship needed. And if the program is STEM-designated, OPT extends to thirty-six months. Three years of legal US work, independent entirely of my green-card timeline.

Which means: even if my EAD is taking forever, even if something goes sideways in my immigration sequence, you have your own independent route to working in the US. From there, an employer can sponsor you for your own H-1B. You'd have your own immigration status — not as my dependent, but as your own person, on your own visa.

The same degree, at a third of the cost, with a built-in path to your own H-1B. The H-4 turns into a launching pad instead of a waiting room.
— The whole argument in one sentence

3. And when we come back to India — that works too.

When we move back to India the studying still works for that.

A US degree in a field you actually care about, whether STEM or not gives you credibility, technical depth, and a network when we go back. UX programs in particular have strong international ecosystems. So do design management, business analytics, computer science. The point is: pick the program that matches the work you want to do regardless of geography. The degree comes with you.
