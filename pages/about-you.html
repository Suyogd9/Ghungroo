Chapter 01 — On You

Six things I keep coming back to.

Specific things, not generic compliments. The kind that stay with a person after the conversation ends.

We have been talking for a little over two months now. Long enough that the awkwardness of the arrangement has worn off, but not so long that I have stopped noticing things. What follows are the six observations I keep returning to.

01

You are, before anything else, an artist.

Bharatanatyam. The Arangetram still ahead of you. The cooking channel with these amazing recipes, and the art channel ani tujhe sundar arts. You called yourself "more of an artistic person" almost as an aside, like it was a confession.

Pan kharach sangu? It wasn't a confession. It was the most observable fact about you, kalakar ahes tu, agdi hadachi.

02

We want the same things.

"Ayushya bhar job nahi karaychai. Want to start something of my own."

I had been thinking the same thing for years, in slightly different words — food industry, restaurant, something with my hands.

But it isn't just the big picture. The small things stack up too.

Work-life balance over salary, both of us. The same allergy to toxic startup hustle culture. The same instinct to settle in Pune eventually, not because we have to, but because we want to. Even the dhol-tasha pathak — me in Shree Ram, tu Vajra-madhye. Same Laxmi Road crowd, same crazy visarjan miravnuk, same goosebumps when the taal picks up.

Apli "same-same" list majhya grocery list peksha mothi jhalie, Saloni. Kahi goshti shodavya lagat nahit; tya samor astat. This connection was meant to be, God's Plan

04

You love your parents fiercely.

The Mother's Day dinner. The retirement gift you surprised your father with. The line that's lived in my head since you said it on a difficult night —

"It's the last thing on earth I'd want to see, them being hurt."

That sentence told me everything I needed to know about how you love.

05

Partnership, not provision.

When I mentioned buying a house for us in Pune, You said —

"Jo kuch bhi karenge, mil ke karenge."

Simple sentence. Big tell.

Most people would have gone with one of the two expected responses. Tu tisra option open kelas, milun karu. That one line told me you don't want to be carried, ani tula carry karaychi hi garaj nahie. Decisions about money, about the house, about the future he aple decisions ahet, mine to make and yours to receive nahit.

That distinction is rare. Most relationships I have watched up close get this wrong in one of two directions, one person carries everything and the other coasts, or one person decides and the other adjusts. Tu pahilyach mahinyat toh tisra option open kelas. Awadlay!

— and one more —
06

The small things, even from this distance.

We met through an arranged setup. We have been chatting for a couple of months, not living a life together. So I haven't earned the inside jokes yet, and there are no shared memories to point to. But even from here across a screen, across EST and IST, the small things have been showing up, ani me te lakshyat thevtoy.

I don't know you fully yet. We are still in the early chapters. Pan ya don mahinyat tujhya baddal ji thodi jhalak milali ahe, tyat sagla artistic, emotional, observant, your love for food, saglach ahe. The portrait is incomplete, obviously. But the strokes I can see are already worth paying attention to.

The conversations have been easy. The silences haven't been awkward. Your "good morning ☀️" arrives at the tail end of my EST day, which turns out to be a decent way to close it. Aturta aste tya message chi nakki.
