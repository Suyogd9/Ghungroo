On you, in six observations

Specific things, not generic compliments. The kind that stay with a person after the conversation ends.

We've been talking for a little over two months now. Long enough that the awkwardness of the anuroop setup has worn off, but not so long that I've stopped noticing things. What follows are the six observations I keep returning to — the ones that won't leave me alone. I've tried to be specific, because anyone can be told they're "kind" or "interesting" and it means nothing.

⁂

i. You are, before anything else, an artist.

Bharatanatyam. The Arangetram still ahead of you. The Instagram channel that hit sixty-four thousand views on a single recipe — shot with a Hollyland mic, voice-over and edit done by you alone. You called yourself "more of an artistic person" almost as an aside, like it was a confession. It wasn't. It was the most observable fact about you.

ii. We want the same things.

"Ayushya bhar job nahi karaychai," you said. "Want to start something of my own." I had been thinking the same thing for years, in slightly different words. There's a kind of relief in finding someone whose north star points in the same direction — you stop having to justify the destination and start talking about the route.

"Ten to seven, bas — tevdhich capacity ahe." You, on work, with no apology in your voice

iii. You hold a sane line on work.

Money for life, not life for money. That was the implicit position, and it was lovely to hear someone state it without performance. Most people pretend to believe this; almost no one actually holds it. You hold it.

iv. You love your parents fiercely.

The Mother's Day dinner you took your mother to. The retirement gift you surprised your father with. The line that's lived in my head since you said it on a difficult night: "It's the last thing on earth I'd want to see — them being hurt." That sentence told me everything I needed to know about how you love.

v. You know your own patterns.

"All days are not same," you said, about your harder days. "Pahile shant aste, mag jel up zalle ki comfortable" — you described yourself with the kind of accuracy most people never achieve about themselves. Self-awareness like that is rare enough that I notice it immediately when it shows up.

vi. The small things accumulate.

The Marathi poem you wrote for Gudi Padwa. The "Disle mala tuze" at the wedding. The "sukhi atma" t-shirt. The lost-keys drama. The single tear-emoji you sent over an unavailable Sps lollipop. Individually, they're nothing. Collectively, they're a portrait.

⁂

What I haven't said often enough: the conversations have been easy. The silences haven't been awkward. Your "good morning 🌞" has been opening my mornings, and your "good night" has been closing them. EST-IST chi maramaar challiye, pan — every minute of it has been worth the sleep I've lost.
