Chapter 02 — My Intent

What I am, in fact,
signing up for.

You called me "khup samjudar." This page is what's behind that word — five things, in descending order of difficulty.

Promises in this format are dangerous, they tend to be either grand and meaningless, or small and condescending. I've tried to keep these specific and verifiable. The first one is the most important.

Your parents become my parents.

Not in the abstract Indian-wedding sense. In the operational sense. The Mother's Day call, the retirement surprise, the worry on the hard nights — those are now my problems too. The line you said — "it's the last thing on earth I'd want to see, them being hurt" — applies to me now also. The complicated family piece, we will work on it patiently, together, on your timeline.

Your work is not on pause after marriage.

The Arangetram. The channel. The business you've been thinking about. None of these get downgraded to hobbies because we got married. The H-4 window is for runway-building. EAD is for takeoff. I want to be your first subscriber, first sponsor lead, and ego aside, first investor when the time comes. The frame I want is: you have a career, I have a career, and we are good for each other's careers.

Partnership, not permission.

Your friend circles, your time, your money, what you study, what you wear, what you collect, these are yours. I get a vote on the things that affect us both; I do not get a veto on the things that are yours. Your alcohol collection — which I still find delightful and slightly mysterious, stays. The space you've built for yourself is the space I want to step into, not the space I want to redecorate.

Direct, always. No games, no guessing.

Naraj asalis tar sang. Khush asalis tar sang. Confuse asalis tar sang. Me tujhe moods overinterpret karnar nahi, pan notice nakkich karin. ("All days are not same", tu mhntalelis na? Lakshyat ahe majhya. Tujhya off divsanmadhye me extra patient asin.)

The boring adult stuff, we handle together.

The H-4 paperwork. The taxes. The insurance. The new-apartment setup. The first six months of adjusting to a country you didn't grow up in. None of this is glamorous, and none of it is something one of us should carry alone. Me lead karen jithe majha experience kami yeil, tu lead karshil jithe tujha approach changla asel. Pan decisions, choices, sagla milunch.

Ani he karta karta — drawing, food channel, dance, parents na calls — tujhya saglya goshtila pan vel milala pahije. Apan balance shodhu, sagla ekatra.

Partnership, not permission. I get a vote, never a veto. The space you've built is the space I want to step into, not redecorate.
— The line above all the others

What this isn't.

This list isn't a contract — it's a starting point. We'll add to it together, edit it together, occasionally violate it together. But an honest starting point is the part most people skip. I wanted you to know mine before we go any further.

I'm sure I'll fail at one of these eventually. Probably promise number four, I overthink. But knowing what I am aiming for makes the failures cheaper to recover from. So: this is the aim.

Ani me clear karto, me 100% perfect mulga nahie. Majhe pan drawbacks ahet, weak points ahet, blind spots jya tula distil pan majhya nazret yenar nahit. Pan me shikaayla, mature whayla, change whayla tayar ahe — for the right reasons, for the right person.

One more thing. Every promise here was easier to write than the one before it. The hardest one is at the top. That's deliberate.
