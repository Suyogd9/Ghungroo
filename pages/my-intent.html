Chapter 02 — My Intent

What I am, in fact,
signing up for.

You called me "khup samjudar." This page is what's behind that word — five things, in descending order of difficulty.

Promises in this format are dangerous — they tend to be either grand and meaningless, or small and condescending. I've tried to keep these specific and verifiable. The first one is the most important.

Your parents become my parents.

Not in the abstract Indian-wedding sense. In the operational sense. The Mother's Day call, the retirement surprise, the worry on the hard nights — those are now my problems too. The line you said — "it's the last thing on earth I'd want to see, them being hurt" — applies to me now also. Pune trips will be frequent. Video calls will be daily. And on the days that ask for more, we'll figure out more. The complicated family piece — we'll work on it patiently, together, on your timeline.

Your work is not on pause after marriage.

The Arangetram. The channel. The business you've been thinking about. None of these get downgraded to hobbies because we got married. The H-4 window is for runway-building. EAD is for takeoff. I want to be your first subscriber, first sponsor lead, and — ego aside — first investor when the time comes. The frame I want is: you have a career, I have a career, and we are good for each other's careers.

Partnership, not permission.

Your friend circles, your time, your money, what you study, what you wear, what you collect — these are yours. I get a vote on the things that affect us both; I do not get a veto on the things that are yours. The ten-to-seven you've drawn around your work life stays. Your alcohol collection — which I still find delightful and slightly mysterious — stays. The space you've built for yourself is the space I want to step into, not the space I want to redecorate.

Direct, always. No games, no guessing.

You said you don't like overly sweet boys. I promise the over-performing politeness of the first month is — well — just the first month. The actual me is more "baat toh sahi hei" than "aap bahut acche ho." If you're upset, please say so. If you're delighted, please say so. If you're unsure, please say so. I won't overinterpret your moods, but I will always notice them. (As you said: "all days are not same." Noted, accepted, baked in.)

The boring adult things are mine to handle.

The H-4 paperwork. The taxes. The insurance. The new-apartment setup. The first six months of adjusting to a country you didn't grow up in. None of this should be your cognitive load. Your job is to write poetry when poetry shows up, to make food when food shows up, to dance when the Arangetram approaches, and to call your parents often. I'll take everything else.

Partnership, not permission. I get a vote, never a veto. The space you've built is the space I want to step into, not redecorate.
— The line above all the others

What this isn't.

This list isn't a contract — it's a starting point. We'll add to it together, edit it together, occasionally violate it together. But an honest starting point is the part most people skip. I wanted you to know mine before we go any further.

I'm sure I'll fail at one of these eventually. Probably promise number four — I overthink. But knowing what I'm aiming for makes the failures cheaper to recover from. So: this is the aim.

One more thing. Every promise here was easier to write than the one before it. The hardest one is at the top. That's deliberate.
