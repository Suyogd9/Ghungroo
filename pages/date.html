Will you go on a date
with me?

Late July through the end of August. Pune. Pick the day; I'll handle the rest. The four chapters before this were all build-up. This is the actual page.

We've talked nearly every day for two months. We've covered childhood, careers, ambitions, anxieties, parents, food, festivals, the small absurd things that make up a person. It has been — by a wide margin — the best thing about this arranged-marriage process for me.

And I'd very much like to meet you in person before we decide anything else. Hence this site. Hence this page. Hence the question.

A few honest expectations.

Dress code: whatever you want.

I don't have a preference and I don't trust anyone who does on a first date. Come comfortable. Come like yourself. The point is meeting you, not auditioning the version of you that fits a dress code.

I'll probably be slightly nervous.

Fair warning. The over-performing politeness from Promise No. 04 may make a brief return. Be patient with me for the first ten minutes. By minute eleven I'll have figured out that you're, in fact, a normal human being and I'll relax.

Please laugh at at least 40% of my jokes.

This is the only firm expectation. Forty percent is generous; my actual hit rate is closer to twenty-five. But the social contract requires a buffer.

Two months of text. One evening in person. That's all I'm asking for — to find out if the conversations we've been having translate into the same easiness when there's no screen in the way.
