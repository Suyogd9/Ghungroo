04 · Your Channel

Your channel — what's allowed when

Short version: you can absolutely start. You can absolutely keep going. Just don't earn from it (yet).

Your 64k-view recipe video already proved you can do this.
Imagine what you'll do with a US kitchen + EAD.
Phase 1 — On plain H-4 (before EAD)

Build the channel. Treat it as a hobby + portfolio.

  • Create and publish videos freely — Instagram, YouTube, both
  • Grow your audience, subscribers, watch hours
  • Do NOT enable monetization (no AdSense, no sponsorships, no paid brand deals, no Super Chats)
  • If you cross 1,000 subs / 4,000 watch hours — congrats, but wait to enable Partner Program until EAD
  • Think of it as building the runway now so you can take off the day EAD arrives
Phase 2 — On H-4 EAD (after I-140)

Switch monetization on. You're now a creator-entrepreneur.

  • Enable YouTube Partner Program → AdSense revenue
  • Accept brand sponsorships and paid collaborations
  • Sell merch, courses, ebooks, digital products
  • Affiliate links (Amazon Associates, etc.)
  • Patreon, memberships, Super Thanks
  • Optional: register an LLC for tax + liability protection
  • File US taxes as self-employed (we'll get a CPA — promise)

Gray area to confirm with a lawyer: running the channel from accounts based outside the US (e.g., monetized to an Indian bank account). Some couples do this; rules are fuzzy. We'll get proper advice before deciding.

Why your channel will probably do better in the US

Production quality jumps

Bigger kitchen, better light, more variety of ingredients, ethnic food scenes in the US are huge — content options multiply.

Audience is global

Your Marathi viewers in the US, UK, Australia are already finding your videos. Living in the US makes "Indian food in the US" a natural niche.

Higher CPM

YouTube pays creators more for US-based viewers. Your channel's revenue per 1000 views could jump 3–5×.

Sponsorships pay in USD

Brand deals from US/global brands typically pay in USD. The math gets really nice, really fast.
